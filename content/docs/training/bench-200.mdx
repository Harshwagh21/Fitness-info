## Introduction

Whether you're gunning for your first **140kg (315lbs) bench** or trying to smash past **200kg (440+)**, the program that gets you there has to evolve as you do.

Let's look at how lifters at different strength levels typically train and why your old routine might be holding you back.

---

## 100–150kg Bench Press (Intermediate to Advanced)

Once you're in this range, the simple magic of linear beginner programs has likely faded. Your "newbie gains" have dried up, and what got you here won't get you there. Progress now demands a much more structured approach.

This is the stage where you'll still see great results from moderate frequency, hypertrophy work, and solid periodization.

### Training Approach

**The focus shifts to:**
- Building your overall work capacity through volume
- Perfecting your technique
- Strengthening your triceps, shoulders, and back with accessory exercises

**Frequency:** Bench **two or three times a week**

**Intensity:** Your main sets should feel like an **RPE of 7–9**, which allows you to use autoregulation to account for daily swings in fatigue and recovery.

**The Science:** Research on trained individuals shows that higher frequencies like this are superior for both strength and hypertrophy compared to training less often, especially when the total weekly volume is matched.

### Recommended Programs

#### 1. Greg Nuckols' 28 Programs (Bench-Focused Version)

- Evidence-based, customizable programs
- Uses a mix of percentages and RPE to manage progression
- Highly adaptable to individual needs

#### 2. Candito 6-Week Strength Program

- Popular periodized program
- Cycles through distinct phases focused on:
  - Power
  - Strength
  - Control

#### 3. ABC DUP-Style Bench (Moderate, Technique, Heavy Split)

A classic Daily Undulating Periodization model that constantly varies intensity and volume.

**Why it works:** DUP has proven to be just as, if not more, effective than linear periodization for strength gains in trained lifters because it provides a varied stimulus that prevents your body from getting too comfortable.

**We've previously covered the ABC DUP-style bench program and still consider it our top pick for most lifters in this range.** To make things even easier, we've included an interactive RPE / %1RM Warmup Planner that tells you exactly which plates to load for every set.

#### 4. 5/3/1 Beyond with Push-Focused Accessories

Jim Wendler's philosophy emphasizes slow, consistent progress over the long haul.

- Highly customizable
- Lets you add the accessory work you need to fix your weak points
- Sustainable long-term approach

#### 5. Sheiko Intermediate Templates

Known for their high volume at submaximal weights.

- Designed to build a huge work capacity
- Hammers in perfect technique
- Excellent for building volume tolerance

#### 6. RTS General Strength Templates (3–4x/week)

Mike Tuchscherer's systems are built on RPE-based autoregulation.

- Ensures your training is always matched to your daily readiness
- Perfectly balances skill work and fatigue
- Highly effective for intermediate lifters

### Why These Approaches Work

You're still building your foundational strength and capacity. Smartly applied volume and consistent technical practice lead to reliable, sustainable gains as your body adapts more slowly than a true beginner's.

## 150–200kg Bench Press (Advanced)

Hitting this level means progress slows down considerably. Pushing forward requires greater specificity, higher frequency, and careful fatigue management. This is where many lifters stall out if they stick with the same old programming.

### Training Approach

**At this point, the bench press becomes much more of a skill that needs frequent practice**, so training it **4 to 5 times a week** becomes common.

**Intensity:** Your working sets will likely hover around an **RPE of 6.5 to 9**, often structured with heavy top sets followed by back-off work to accumulate volume.

**Precise autoregulation is no longer a suggestion; it's a necessity.**

**The focus narrows to:**
- Achieving extreme bar path efficiency
- Mastering pause control for raw lifters
- Aggressively attacking your specific weak points (like a slow lockout or weak chest drive)

**Advanced Methods:**
Some lifters find success with **Low-Load, High-Volume (LL-HV)** or other high-frequency approaches. In fact, one study suggests LL-HV training can boost performance and structural adaptations in powerlifters, potentially improving bar speed on submaximal lifts and even increasing arm circumference.

### Recommended Programs

#### 1. Bilbo Bench Program

Rayan "Bilbo" Bellkhir's method revolves around very high frequency, often daily, with tons of volume using submaximal loads.

**Key features:**
- Heavily emphasizes paused and tempo reps
- Refines your bar path
- Improves bar speed
- High frequency, submaximal approach

#### 2. RTS Bench Templates

These programs continue to use RPE-based autoregulation but often add:
- Top singles for neural drive
- Back-off sets tailored to build volume
- Address specific weaknesses

#### 3. Custom Peaking Blocks

These are designed around a competition date.

- Strategically lower volume while ramping up intensity
- Ensures you're at your absolute strongest on meet day
- Highly individualized

#### 4. Sheiko Advanced Programs

More complex versions of the intermediate templates.

- Maintain high submaximal volume
- Focus on technical perfection during accumulation phases
- Before you peak

#### 5. Smolov Jr.

A brutal, short-term specialization cycle for the bench press.

- Can produce rapid strength gains
- Intensity isn't sustainable for long periods
- Should be used with caution

> **💡 Note:** We plan to cover these advanced methods in future posts.

### Why This Training Works

You're no longer just lifting a weight; you're executing a highly developed skill. Higher frequencies and focused variations help ingrain perfect motor patterns, while advanced fatigue management keeps you from burning out.

## 200kg+ Bench Press (Elite)

Benching over **200kg raw** places you in a truly elite class of lifters. Here, gains are measured in tiny increments and demand unbelievable consistency, meticulous planning, and often, the eye of a dedicated coach.

### Training Approach

**To push numbers at this level, you need to treat the bench press as a daily practice**, hitting it **5 to 6 times a week** to maximize skill acquisition and total exposure.

**Volume:** Extremely high but has to be regulated with surgical precision. While you're doing a lot of reps, the intensity of any single set might be lower to manage the crushing accumulated fatigue.

**Intensity:** RPE is now tightly controlled between **6.5 and 9.5**, with elite lifters relying on it to fine-tune every session based on their daily readiness to avoid overreaching.

**Progression:**
- Shifts to microloading with tiny weight jumps
- Strategic top singles
- Sophisticated deloads

**Recovery:** Managing central nervous system (CNS) fatigue becomes just as important as managing muscular fatigue, often requiring advanced recovery protocols.

### Recommended Programs

The programs and approaches used are almost always **highly individualized**:

#### 1. Bilbo (Paid or Replicated Templates)

Continued use of Rayan Bellkhir's high-frequency, submaximal velocity, and paused rep system, usually customized for the individual.

#### 2. RTS Custom

Fully individualized programs developed with:
- Direct athlete feedback
- Advanced fatigue tracking
- Sometimes using velocity-based training alongside RPE

#### 3. JP Cauchi, Chad Wesley Smith Methods

These coaches are known for creating:
- Highly specific, intensity-driven training blocks
- Periodized programs designed to peak elite lifters
- For major competitions

#### 4. Bulgarian-Style Benching

While more common for squats, this is an incredibly demanding approach that involves:
- Hitting daily or near-daily maximal singles
- Requires exceptional recovery and monitoring
- Not for everyone

#### 5. Sheiko Advanced, Customized for Meet Prep

Tailored versions of Sheiko's complex programs, modified to fit an elite lifter's specific needs and competition schedule.

### Why This Training Works

At the elite level, you're chasing fractional gains with extreme precision. The bench press is a refined craft, and every variable—volume, intensity, fatigue, and recovery—must be managed perfectly to squeeze out another fraction of a kilogram on your PR.

## Quick Summary

| Bench Range | Frequency | Key Focus | Typical Programs |
|-------------|----------|-----------|------------------|
| **100–150kg** | 2–3x/week | Strength + hypertrophy | ABC split, Nuckols, 5/3/1, Candito |
| **150–200kg** | 4–5x/week | Skill + specificity + recovery | Bilbo, RTS, Sheiko Adv., custom peaking blocks |
| **200kg+** | 5–6x/week | Precision + autoregulation | Bilbo, RTS Custom, Bulgarian, coached |

---

## Key Principles Across All Levels

### 1. Frequency Increases with Strength

- **100-150kg:** 2-3x/week is sufficient
- **150-200kg:** 4-5x/week becomes necessary
- **200kg+:** 5-6x/week for skill refinement

### 2. Autoregulation Becomes Critical

As you advance, RPE-based training becomes essential to match daily readiness and prevent overreaching.

### 3. Specificity Increases

The closer you get to your max, the more specific your training needs to be.

### 4. Recovery Management

Advanced lifters must manage both muscular and CNS fatigue with sophisticated recovery protocols.

### 5. Individualization

Elite lifters almost always need customized programs tailored to their specific needs and weaknesses.

---

## Key Takeaways

✅ **Your program must evolve** - What got you to 100kg won't get you to 200kg

✅ **Frequency increases with strength** - More advanced lifters need more practice

✅ **Autoregulation is essential** - RPE-based training becomes critical

✅ **Specificity matters more** - Weak points must be aggressively targeted

✅ **Recovery is paramount** - Both muscular and CNS fatigue must be managed

✅ **Consider coaching** - Elite lifters often benefit from expert guidance

✅ **Be patient** - Progress slows dramatically at advanced levels

You don't need to live in the gym when you're benching 100kg. But once you start chasing 150kg, 200kg, and beyond, things like specificity, advanced fatigue management, and refined recovery become far more important than just raw effort.
