## Introduction

Protein is essential, but it isn't free. It costs money and puts a real load on your organs, which is why your goal should be to **consume just enough for your goals, not as much as possible**.

---

## Common Recommendations (And Their Limitations)

You've probably heard the common recommendations. Current evidence points to **1.2–2.0 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight** as plenty for most active people.

**Other popular rules:**
- Jeff Nippard's suggestion: **1 gram of protein per centimeter of height**
- Old gym standby: **2 grams per kilogram** for building a better physique

### The Problem with Simple Rules

**But it's not quite that simple.**

Those rules are a decent starting point, but they miss the critical details. Your actual protein needs change constantly depending on the context.

**Here's a perfect example that might sound backward at first:** Ronnie Coleman, during a bulking phase, could actually require **less protein per kilogram of lean mass** than an average person who's trying to lose weight in a calorie deficit.

### Why Research Gives Ranges

When you dig into the research papers, you'll notice they always give a **range for protein intake, not a single magic number**. The real challenge is figuring out exactly where you fall within that recommended range.

---

## Key Factors That Determine Your Protein Needs

Several key factors determine your specific protein needs:

### 1. Training Intensity and Frequency

**How hard and how often you're training** directly impacts your protein requirements.

- More intense training = higher protein needs
- Higher training frequency = higher protein needs
- Resistance training = higher needs than cardio alone

### 2. Energy Balance (Calorie Surplus vs. Deficit)

**Whether you're in a calorie surplus to gain weight or a deficit to lose it** significantly affects protein needs.

- **Calorie deficit:** Higher protein needs (to preserve muscle)
- **Calorie surplus:** Lower protein needs (easier to maintain muscle)
- **Maintenance:** Moderate protein needs

### 3. Body Fat Percentage

**Your current body fat percentage** matters because protein needs should be based on lean mass, not total weight.

- Higher body fat = less lean mass = different protein calculation
- Lower body fat = more lean mass = different protein calculation

### 4. Fat-Free Mass Index (FFMI)

**Your Fat-Free Mass Index (FFMI)** accounts for your lean mass relative to your height.

- Higher FFMI = more muscle = potentially different protein needs
- Lower FFMI = less muscle = different protein needs

---

## The Dynamic Nature of Protein Requirements

Simple rules like **1.6–2.2 g/kg** or **1 g/cm of height** are fine for a general idea, but you have to remember that **your protein requirement is dynamic**.

**For a much more accurate approach:**
- Base your intake on your **lean body mass** instead of your total body weight
- Adjust that number based on your **energy balance**
- Consider your **training demands**
- Factor in **how lean you are**

## Optimal Protein Intake by Goal

| Goal | Protein Intake | Notes |
|------|----------------|-------|
| **Body Recomp** | 2.0 - 2.5 g/kg of Lean Body Mass (LBM) per day | Building muscle while losing fat |
| **Bodybuilding Cut** | 2.3 - 3.1 g/kg of Lean Body Mass (LBM) per day | Aggressive deficit, preserve muscle |
| **Lean Bulk** | 1.6 - 2.2 g/kg of Total Body Weight per day | Moderate surplus, muscle gain |
| **Underweight Bulk** | 1.6 - 2.2 g/kg of Lean Body Mass (LBM) per day | Reaching healthy weight |
| **Weight Loss** | 1.8 - 2.2 g/kg of Lean Body Mass (LBM) per day | General fat loss |

### Why These Ranges Matter

**Body Recomp (2.0-2.5 g/kg LBM):**
- Higher needs because you're trying to build muscle while losing fat
- Based on lean body mass, not total weight

**Bodybuilding Cut (2.3-3.1 g/kg LBM):**
- Highest needs because you're in an aggressive deficit
- Must preserve muscle mass while losing fat
- Based on lean body mass

**Lean Bulk (1.6-2.2 g/kg Total Body Weight):**
- Lower needs because you're in a surplus
- Easier to maintain muscle when eating more calories
- Based on total body weight (convenience)

**Underweight Bulk (1.6-2.2 g/kg LBM):**
- Moderate needs for healthy weight gain
- Based on lean body mass for accuracy

**Weight Loss (1.8-2.2 g/kg LBM):**
- Moderate-high needs to preserve muscle
- Based on lean body mass

---

## Why Lean Body Mass Matters

**The key difference:** Most recommendations use total body weight, but **lean body mass is more accurate** because:

- Fat tissue doesn't need protein for maintenance
- Muscle tissue is what requires protein
- Two people of the same weight but different body fat % have very different protein needs

**Example:**
- Person A: 90 kg, 12% body fat = 79.2 kg LBM
- Person B: 90 kg, 30% body fat = 63 kg LBM

Using total weight would give them the same protein target, but Person A (more muscle) actually needs more protein.
